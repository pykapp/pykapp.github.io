*people you know* made a handful of decisions the other way round from most
photo apps, and you meet them on the first day. These pages say what the app
does and why, in enough detail to predict what will happen before you tap.

**Last updated:** 12 September 2026. They describe the closed beta on Android.

If you have used Instagram or WhatsApp for years, start with
[what works differently here](/how-it-works/different/). It is the short list
of places where an instinct from another app will be wrong.

## The pages

- [Posting a photo](/how-it-works/posting/): choosing photographs, writing a
  caption, and choosing who a post goes to.
- [Connecting with people](/how-it-works/people/): invitations, add requests,
  the limit on how many people you can be connected to, and what removing or
  blocking somebody does.
- [Groups](/how-it-works/groups/): private labels for sets of the people you
  are connected to, which the people in them are never told about.
- [Albums](/how-it-works/albums/): a shared album that several people add to,
  and how it differs from a group.
- [The feed](/how-it-works/feed/): a queue that empties, what makes a post
  leave it, and what the app will and will not notify you about.
- [Comments and reactions](/how-it-works/comments/): who can see a comment,
  which is not everybody who can see the photograph.
- [How the privacy works](/how-it-works/privacy/): the keys, the recovery
  phrase, what our servers hold, and what they cannot open.

## The rest of this site

The [privacy policy](/privacy/) is the formal statement of what we hold and
what we cannot see; the page above is the mechanism underneath it. The
[terms of use](/terms/) say what you agree to, and the
[moderation policy](/moderation/) says what we can act on when we cannot see
what was posted. [Deleting your account](/delete-account/) has a page of its
own, because you may be asking without the app.

If something here is wrong, or the app does something these pages do not
explain, *settings → report a bug* goes straight to us.
